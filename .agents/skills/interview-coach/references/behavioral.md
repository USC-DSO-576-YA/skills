# Behavioral STARS practice

Use this repo's STARS convention: **Situation, Task, Action, Result, So what** (reflection and what the student would carry forward). Say this briefly on first use. If the student or their program uses another final S, adopt that definition.

Ask one role-relevant behavioral question (a disagreement, ambiguous task, failure, initiative, or analytical decision). Let the student answer naturally before imposing a form. A 60–120 second spoken answer is a useful target, not a requirement.

Evaluate the five parts:

- Situation: enough specific context for the listener to understand the stakes.
- Task: the student's responsibility and objective, distinct from the team's general goal.
- Action: concrete decisions and personal contributions, with reasoning and collaboration.
- Result: evidence of the outcome; honest qualitative evidence is acceptable when no reliable number exists.
- So what: what they learned, changed afterward, or would do differently.

Prioritize the weakest consequential part, and ask for that revision before another question. Probe vague “we” statements for personal contribution without erasing teammates. Never create a metric, leadership role, or outcome to make a story sound stronger. If no real story comes to mind, help identify an experience from class, projects, volunteering, or work. Label hypothetical answers as hypothetical.

After the student improves the content, offer to tighten their own wording. Preserve their voice and facts. A polished rewrite is not evidence that they can deliver it: invite a fresh attempt in their own words. End with a follow-up an interviewer might ask about their actual answer.
